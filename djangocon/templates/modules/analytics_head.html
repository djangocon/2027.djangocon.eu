{% comment %} Google Analytics 4, behind consent. Goes near the top of the document head, per Google's gtag.js install guide ("immediately after the opening head tag"), so that a returning visitor who has already accepted starts measuring as early as possible instead of waiting for the end of the body. There is deliberately no noscript fallback: that belongs to Google Tag Manager, whose noscript iframe still fires tags without JS. GA4's gtag.js measures purely in JavaScript, so a noscript block could not report anything and would be dead markup. The privacy guide tells visitors they can control non-essential cookies from a banner on first visit, and GDPR/ePrivacy require consent before analytics cookies are set. So gtag.js is not requested at all until the visitor accepts: no request reaches Google and no cookie is written on a first load or after a refusal. The banner itself is in modules/analytics_banner.html. The choice is kept in localStorage rather than a cookie -- it never needs to reach the server, and a cookie asking for consent to cookies is a poor look. Notes on the script below, kept here rather than inline because inline comments are served to the browser and show up in view-source: * read() swallows a localStorage failure (private mode, storage blocked) and returns null, i.e. undecided: the banner reappears next visit rather than the site tracking silently. * gtag is assigned to window because gtag.js looks it up there, and called through the local alias so the calls do not rely on an implicit global. * Traffic source, session duration and page-to-page navigation all come from GA4's own page_view and session handling; no custom events are needed. * A previous "accept" is honoured on load, before the banner script runs. {% endcomment %}
