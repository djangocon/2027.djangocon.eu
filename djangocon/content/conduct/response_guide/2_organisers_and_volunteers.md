Every organizer and volunteer represents DjangoCon Europe's commitment to the <a href="/conduct/code_of_conduct/" class="pages-links">Code of Conduct</a>, and participants notice how staff behave and respond.

If you witness an incident or receive a report:

- Find a CARE team member to take over as soon as possible — look for CARE badges, or check the shared on-call schedule.
- If a CARE member isn't immediately available, document what you can: what happened, who was involved, when and where, whether it's ongoing, and the reporter's contact information, if they're willing to share it.
- Encourage the reporter to also email <a href="mailto:conduct@djangocon.eu" class="pages-links">conduct@djangocon.eu</a>, so there's a written record independent of your notes.
- Pass what you've documented to a CARE member promptly, and only to CARE. Do not discuss the details with other staff, speakers, or attendees.
- Stay compassionate and attentive, but do not promise a specific outcome, consequence, or timeline; that's CARE's decision after review.
- If the reporter consents, you may arrange an escort back to their room or a public area, help them contact a friend, or call for medical or emergency services. Never do these things without the reporter's consent unless there is immediate danger to life.

### Unilateral intervention

In genuinely urgent situations — ongoing physical violence, immediate danger, or a severe violation happening live during a talk — any staff member may act immediately, including asking someone to leave or contacting Austrian emergency services (112 / 133 / 144), without waiting for CARE.

Consult a CARE member as soon as it's safe to do so afterward.
