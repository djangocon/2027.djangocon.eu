The Code of Conduct Active Response Ensurers (CARE) handle reports from receipt through resolution and follow-up.

### Receiving a report

- Acknowledge receipt promptly and compassionately. Do not imply a conclusion about whether a violation occurred, or what the outcome will be, at this stage.
- Log the report in the shared CARE incident record. Access is restricted to CARE members; see the <a href="/conduct/privacy_guide/" class="pages-links">privacy guide</a> for retention.

### Working the case

- CARE decides and acts collectively wherever possible. If immediate action is needed and the full team isn't reachable, one member may act, but should consult at least one other CARE member as soon as practical afterward.
- Determine what happened, whether it violates the Code of Conduct, and who is responsible.
- Consider context: was the behavior a one-off, part of a pattern, intentional, or a misunderstanding? Prior reports about the same person, if any, are relevant context, not automatic escalation.
- Decide on a resolution. Options include, in increasing order of severity: no action, if no violation is found; a private conversation and a request to stop specific behavior; a formal warning; a no-contact requirement between the parties for the remainder of the conference; withholding or removing a talk recording; cancelling a scheduled talk; or removing someone from the conference without refund.
- "No action" is a legitimate outcome, not a failure of process. It should be reached and communicated as deliberately as any other resolution.

### Conflicts of interest

CARE members must declare a conflict — a close personal or professional relationship with either party — as soon as they recognize it, and recuse themselves from that case's decision-making.

### Communicating outcomes

- Tell the reporter what action was taken and, at a level of detail that respects the other party's privacy, why.
- Avoid public statements naming individuals, except where the incident itself happened publicly and silence would undermine confidence in the process. Any public statement should be reviewed by more than one CARE member first.
- After the conference, publish an anonymized transparency report: number of reports received, general categories, and resolutions, without identifying details.
