title: communications protocol during an active incident
layout: simple
order: 4

This is new for 2027 and addresses a gap in prior editions: how information about an unfolding incident is managed in real time, not just reported on afterward.

- Only CARE — or, in urgent cases, whoever is coordinating with local authorities — speaks on behalf of the conference about an active incident. Other staff and volunteers should not speculate, confirm, or deny details on <a href="https://join.slack.com/t/djangoconeurope/shared_invite/zt-340erqj3r-s5ekP4aYz95jv14GZMPCAg" target="\_blank" class="pages-links">Slack</a>, in person, or on social media, even if asked directly.
- If a report becomes visible to a wider group before CARE has reviewed it — for example, something happened in view of many attendees — CARE should issue a short, factual holding statement as soon as possible, acknowledging that something is being looked into, without naming anyone or speculating about outcome, rather than leaving a vacuum that gets filled by rumor on Slack or social media.
- Set an internal expectation for how quickly an update is owed to affected parties. For example: acknowledgment within 2 hours during conference days, a substantive update within 24 hours, even if the case isn't resolved yet. Silence reads as inaction even when work is happening.
- Do not discuss an active case in the general organizer Slack channel; use a private CARE-only channel.
